⚠ Storm alert for your town

Severe weather is expected

Do these now

  • Charge phones and backup batteries
  • Fill water jugs — and the bathtub
  • Set out flashlights (not candles)
  • Make sure you have a week of medications
  • Secure or haul boats; double your lines
  • Bring in anything that can blow away
  • Have a generator? Check fuel and give it a test run

If the power goes out

  • Keep fridge and freezer doors closed
  • Never run a generator indoors or in a garage
  • Stay far away from downed lines — call 911
  • Check on your nearest neighbor

Wildfire Risk

The state forestry agency rates fire danger daily during fire season (spring through fall): Low, Moderate, High, Very High, or Extreme. At Very High or Extreme, burn permits are often suspended — check the map below before burning brush or lighting a campfire.

Ticks can be active on any day above about 40°F, and mosquitoes can bite on days rated Low. A low estimate is never a reason to skip precautions during a walk in tall grass or brush.

How these estimates work — and how well

Each meter combines today's hourly forecast (temperature, humidity, and wind) with the seasonal calendar for a northern temperate climate (see the README to adjust it for your latitude). Mosquitoes are scored on the dusk hours, when they bite most; ticks on the daytime hours. Temperature sets the ceiling, and dry air or wind pulls the estimate down. The seasonal calendar follows the deer tick life cycle — the April–July nymph peak (when most Lyme transmission happens) and the October–November adult season — and the June–August mosquito peak.

What we checked: ten seasonal scenarios (January cold snap, July heat wave, a breezy dry summer day, mild October afternoon, and so on) were run through the model and compared against standard vector-ecology guidance. An earlier version of the model failed this test and was corrected. Expect the meter to run slightly hot on humid summer days.

What this can't tell you: weather is only part of the story. These estimates know nothing about actual insect populations near your house — standing water, deer traffic, leaf litter, or how wet the past month was all matter and aren't in the model. For real surveillance, use the sources below.
